Practical questions

What is the machine doing, and how would we know?

Students can compare prompts, inspect patterns, test image or text outputs, collect confident mistakes, and connect machine decisions to game and robot behaviour.

The important habit is verification: asking what evidence supports an answer, what data may be missing, and what a person remains responsible for deciding.

Future project direction

Small experiments that expose the limits.

TEST_01

Is This AI Guessing?

Ask related questions, classify the answers, and investigate whether confidence tracks correctness.

TEST_02

Confidently Wrong Machine

Build a collection of plausible errors and design a checklist for checking machine-generated claims.

TEST_03

Robot Chooses a Snack

Create simple decision rules, compare them with learned patterns, and debate what makes a choice sensible.

Responsible use

A prompt is not understanding.

AI can help generate possibilities, images, text, or patterns. It can also reproduce errors, hide uncertainty, and make weak ideas sound polished.

Students should be able to explain what they asked, what they received, what they checked, what they changed, and which decisions remain their own.
