## Project workbench detail

```text
Use case: photorealistic-natural
Asset type: School of Code project-workbench campaign master for Projects, Lessons, blog cards, and responsive detail crops
Primary request: Create one close, energetic, text-free photorealistic image of a real maker workbench in active use. Show only two pairs of natural hands entering from different edges while testing and adjusting one strange but mechanically credible working mechanism. Base the machine closely on the real improvised apparatus in Images 1–5.
Input images: Image 1 is the primary relay-board, coloured test-lead, reused-container and taped-wire reference. Image 2 supports the two-vessel rig, bench supply, controller and diagnostic arrangement. Image 3 supports the improvised containers, copper parts and unruly wiring. Image 4 supports real tool scale, ordinary tabletop energy and hands-on repair context; do not include or recreate the child's face. Image 5 supports the eccentric long wrapped sensor/coil and transparent-vessel construction language.
Scene/backdrop: A modest warm wooden or pale laminate workbench in a compact community maker room. The background is shallow-focus and secondary, with no institution or facility claim.
Subject: A peculiar working machine assembled from a shallow tray, two clear reused containers, copper coil, small wheels, a relay/controller board, one sensor, improvised brackets, a small motor, purposeful red/green/yellow test leads, tape, mismatched fasteners and one slightly ridiculous moving appendage. One hand steadies a wheel or bracket; one uses a small screwdriver; another holds a multimeter probe or test lead; the fourth hand sorts or offers a component. Every wire terminates plausibly and every tool contacts a real fastener or test point.
Style/medium: High-fidelity candid editorial campaign photography, not a stock-photo setup. Preserve real material texture, scuffs, fingerprints, wood grain, imperfect tape, solder, wire bends and ordinary tool wear.
Composition/framing: Wide 3:2 landscape master with an energetic diagonal mechanism across the centre. Keep all important hands, tools and mechanism comfortably inside the frame. Provide crop flexibility for 16:9, 4:5, square and small horizontal cards. No faces.
Lighting/mood: Warm natural window light with one practical bench lamp; intelligent, playful, active and slightly chaotic, but safe and not dirty.
Color palette: Natural wood, charcoal, copper and clear materials with restrained School of Code accents appearing only as real objects—yellow component tray, green test lead, small blue/red/violet parts.
Constraints: Exactly four visible hands belonging to two people; no faces or identifiable people. Natural anatomy and fingers. Mechanically continuous wheels, brackets, sensors, motor, relay board, tools and wires. No readable text, labels, numbers, logos, badges, commercial brands, institutional signs, emblems, watermarks, screens, futuristic effects or fake holograms. The image must remain truthfully connected to the real source machines without implying a luxury lab.
Avoid: Generic robot kit, pristine plastic robot, coordinated staged hands, floating components, impossible wiring, extra fingers, cropped fingertips at the frame edge, sparks, unsafe bare mains wiring, neon, corporate blue wash, luxury laboratory, generated writing or branding.
```
