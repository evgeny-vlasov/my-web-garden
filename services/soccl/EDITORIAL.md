# School of Code editorial system

The publishing desk serves a small, hands-on Calgary computer lab. Its job is
not to keep feeds full at any cost. Its job is to help useful, concrete ideas
reach parents without turning the school into generic educational marketing.

## Publishing rhythm

All scheduling uses Calgary / Mountain Time.

- Facebook: three distinct posts each day — morning, noon, and evening.
- Instagram: one visual-first post each day.
- Website: one substantial article every five days.

The Facebook rhythm is deliberately varied:

- morning: a useful idea, parent question, short explanation, or observation;
- noon: a project, bug, teaching moment, image, or workbench detail;
- evening: a discussion, story, opinion, genuine question, or article link.

A website article may generate several distinct social posts. Most social posts
do not need to become articles. If a slot has no worthwhile idea, use a strong
evergreen item or leave the slot empty rather than manufacture filler.

## Editorial gate

Every content package records two things before drafting:

1. **Reason to exist:** the question, tension, complication, useful example,
   surprising detail, or practical gift that makes the item worth publishing.
2. **Editorial sentence:** the one sentence that every part of the package must
   serve.

Before approval, check for one clear idea, a concrete detail, honest evidence
boundaries, Eugene's natural voice, and a useful gift to the reader. Reject
synthetic enthusiasm, engagement bait, vague benefits, and content whose only
reason to exist is the schedule.

## Proportional depth

### Routine social — light pass

Use one clear idea, one concrete detail, and a natural reason to read. Keep
fact and opinion distinct. Instagram begins with a real visual subject and gets
its own concise caption rather than copied Facebook text.

### Factual or controversial social — full evidence check

Record reliable evidence, unresolved questions, classroom experience,
Eugene's opinion, and the strongest reasonable counterargument. AI, screen
time, Roblox, research claims, and educational controversy are amber by
default.

### Website article — full process

Investigate the topic, verify claims with reliable sources, preserve uncertainty,
write the editorial sentence, and remove material that does not serve it. The
result should be durable and useful rather than a marketing page or news recap.

## Approval levels

- **Green:** routine, non-sensitive content that can follow the normal queue.
- **Amber:** factual, research-based, controversial, or otherwise deserving a
  deliberate review.
- **Red:** identifiable children, personal stories, strong developmental claims,
  or sensitive controversy. A person must explicitly approve it.

The current service records these levels and enforces the editorial gate before
a draft can enter review. Automatic generation and external channel delivery
are separate future capabilities; neither should bypass this policy.
